## The Story

Every content team I worked with had the same expensive habit. Before writing a single word of a real article, a strategist or writer would spend three to five hours doing the research - twenty open tabs, six half-read Reddit threads, four Substack essays, three whitepapers, one YouTube video played at 2x. Then they'd try to synthesize all of it into a coherent point of view. By the time the actual writing started, half a day was already gone.

The tools that promised to fix this - "AI writers" that spit out a full article from a prompt - produced generic mush. Real research needs to loop. Read a source, decide what's still missing, search again, read more, then finally form a view. Nothing on the market did that at a price a content team could actually run.

## What I Built

I built them a research agent that does the looping for them. A strategist drops in a topic and a target angle. The agent goes out, pulls real sources, reads them, decides what it still doesn't understand, searches again, reads more, and keeps going until it has a coherent view. Then it hands back a structured research brief - the strongest arguments each side is making, the specific data points worth citing, the gaps other content on the topic keeps missing, and a suggested angle the strategist can use as the article's spine. The full source list drops into a doc so the writer can cite properly.

The writer doesn't start from zero. They start with a thesis worth writing about.

## The Outcome

Three-to-five hours of pre-writing research collapsed to about five minutes of reading the brief. Content velocity roughly doubled for the teams that rolled it out. And the strange side effect - every writer mentioned this - was that the articles ended up sharper than usual, because the research was more thorough than what they used to do by hand at 4 PM on a Thursday.
