## The Story

Every startup ops person I met dreaded the same email. "Hi, submitting my expenses for the month." Attached: fifteen receipt photos taken on a phone, a screenshot of an Uber summary, a PDF invoice, and one blurry paper receipt they photographed in a taxi. Then the same conversation, twenty times a month, across a growing team. Whose card? What category? Client-billable or internal? Approved or not?

The finance person was spending days on data entry. Employees were waiting weeks for reimbursement. And half the receipts were getting misfiled or forgotten, which meant real money the company could've written off just quietly evaporated.

## What I Built

I built them an expense worker that lives at the submission endpoint. An employee just forwards a receipt - from their inbox, from Slack, from their phone - and the workflow reads the image or PDF, pulls out the vendor, amount, date, tax, and payment method, and categorizes it against the company's actual expense policy. If it's clearly approved, it goes straight into the sheet and pings the employee "logged, reimbursement queued." If it needs a human's eye - over-limit, uncategorized vendor, missing info - it flags finance in Slack with the context and a suggested category.

The employee submits and forgets. Finance stops typing and starts approving.

## The Outcome

Days of month-end data entry compressed to minutes of approval work. Reimbursement time to employees dropped from weeks to same-day for clean submissions. Cost per receipt processed is a fraction of a cent. And the "lost receipts" problem - the one that costs companies real deductions every year - mostly disappeared, because losing a receipt now requires actively deleting the email.
