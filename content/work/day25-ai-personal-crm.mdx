## The Story

Every solo founder and consultant I met carried the same guilt. Somewhere in their inbox was a conversation from four months ago with someone they *should* have followed up with. A prospect who said "let's talk in Q3." A former client who'd asked to be pinged when the new offering launched. An investor who'd asked for a quarterly update. All of them, buried in a Gmail thread nobody remembered to search.

Real CRMs - HubSpot, Salesforce - solve this in theory. In practice, solo operators never keep them updated. Manually logging every conversation, every follow-up date, every promise made - nobody does it. So the relationships that were supposed to compound over time just quietly ghosted.

## What I Built

I built them a CRM that fills itself in from their inbox. The workflow watches Gmail, and every time a conversation that matters comes through - a real prospect, a former client, a warm intro - it reads the thread, extracts the person, the company, the context, the promises made ("I'll circle back in three weeks"), and drops it all into a personal relationship database. Follow-up dates get set automatically from the language of the email. When a follow-up date lands, Slack pings the founder with the context: who they are, what was said, what was promised, and a suggested opener.

The founder stops maintaining a CRM. Their inbox does it silently in the background.

## The Outcome

Follow-ups that used to slip stopped slipping - because Slack now told the founder about them, in context, on the right day. Relationships that would've gone cold got a warm nudge instead. Cost per email processed is a fraction of a cent. And the founders using it started closing "old lead" deals that would've quietly died in the inbox otherwise.
