## The Story

Every early-stage founder I met had the same guilty admission — they were three months behind on investor updates. Not because they didn't want to send them. Because writing a monthly investor update is deceptively hard. Pull the metrics, format them, remember which wins matter, remember which asks are outstanding, write it in a confident tone that isn't over-promising, get it out by the fifth. That's a half-day of a founder's time that could've been spent selling or building.

So it slips. One month, then two, then investors start pinging privately asking "how's the business going?" — which is the exact conversation the update was supposed to prevent.

## What I Built

I built them an update generator that produces the whole thing in about two minutes. The founder drops in the raw numbers — revenue, new customers, cash position, key wins from the month — and the workflow assembles a clean, investor-ready update. Metrics section formatted. Highlights written in the founder's tone. Lowlights framed honestly without being dramatic. Asks section pulling from a running list. Everything laid out the way seasoned investors expect to read it, ready to send from Gmail or archive in Notion for the record.

The founder stops dreading the fifth of the month. The update goes out on time. Investors stop feeling like they have to ask.

## The Outcome

The monthly ritual dropped from a half-day of a founder's time to about ten minutes. Cost per update is a fraction of a cent. Investor sentiment — the thing that quietly determines whether they lead the next round — stayed positive because the founder finally became "the CEO who sends updates on time." And the update history archived in Notion turned into a surprisingly useful founder journal.
