## The Story

Every sales team I looked at had the same silent revenue leak. Reps made cold calls all day. Some picked up. Most didn't. But the follow-up — the email that goes out after the call, the one that references the conversation or the voicemail, the one that actually converts — was the piece that kept getting skipped. A rep would hang up, think "I'll send that follow-up in a minute," and by the end of the day have twenty calls' worth of follow-ups still not sent.

Sales managers watched it happen and couldn't fix it. The reps weren't lazy — they were just doing the next call, because the next call felt more productive than typing an email. But the calls that converted almost always converted because of the follow-up, not the call itself.

## What I Built

I built them a follow-up agent that fires the second a call ends. When the rep hangs up, the workflow catches the call metadata — who they called, how long it lasted, whether it connected or hit voicemail — and writes a personalized follow-up email tuned to what actually happened on that call. Connected call with real interest? The email references specific things the prospect said and proposes a next step. Voicemail? The email is a warm nudge with a value-first offer, not a "just following up on my call." Not connected at all? A short pattern-interrupt.

The rep dials the next number. The follow-up is already on its way, logged in the CRM, tracked in the sheet.

## The Outcome

Follow-up rate on cold calls went from something like thirty percent to essentially a hundred percent — because the follow-up now happened automatically, not "when the rep had a minute." Reply rates jumped because the emails referenced the specific call. Cost per follow-up is a fraction of a cent. And the sales manager finally stopped nagging reps about "did you send the follow-up?"
