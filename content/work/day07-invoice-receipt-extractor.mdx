## The Story

Every freelancer and small-team ops person I met had a shoebox problem. Not a literal shoebox anymore — now it's a Gmail folder called "receipts" with three hundred unopened PDFs. Every month, someone has to sit down for two to six hours and manually type each one into a spreadsheet. Vendor name, amount, tax, date, category. Line by line. Just to close the books.

The cost of getting it wrong is real. Missed receipts mean missed tax deductions. Missed invoices mean missed reimbursements. And the tools that promise to fix this — Dext, Hubdoc — start at fifty a month and top out over three hundred, which is a hard sell when you're already paying an accountant hourly to do the same work anyway.

## What I Built

I built a bookkeeping worker that lives inside their inbox. The moment an invoice or receipt lands as an attachment, it's grabbed, read, and understood. The AI pulls out ten-plus fields — vendor, amount, tax, due date, payment status, category — and drops a clean row into their Google Sheet automatically. If the team has a finance Slack channel, a short summary of the expense pings there so nothing hides.

They stop thinking about receipts. Receipts turn themselves into rows.

## The Outcome

The two-to-six-hour monthly bookkeeping ritual dropped to zero. Cost per extraction is a fraction of a cent — cheaper than the electricity to open the PDF manually. Their accountant now spends billable hours on real advice instead of data entry. And the "receipts folder" that used to be a wall of guilt just sorts itself, quietly, in the background.
