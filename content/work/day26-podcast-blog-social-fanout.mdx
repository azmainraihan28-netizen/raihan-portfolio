## The Story

Every podcaster I met was leaving eighty percent of their content on the table. They'd spend an hour recording, another hour editing, publish the episode — and that was it. The blog version never got written. The social clips never got cut. The email newsletter never referenced the episode. Every episode was a one-shot piece of content in a world that rewards fanout.

The agencies that did it manually charged three to five hundred dollars per episode just for the repurposing, and even they were slow — a single episode's blog post plus five social posts took a full day of a content person's time. So the podcaster either paid a lot, waited a lot, or shipped the episode and moved on.

## What I Built

I built them a fanout pipeline that turns one episode into a full week of content in under fifteen minutes. They drop in the audio file and the workflow transcribes the whole thing, then hands the transcript to an editor that pulls out the three or four sharpest moments, writes a full blog post in the host's voice, generates a LinkedIn post, a Twitter thread, three short-form video hook scripts, an email newsletter version, and a set of pull-quote images with the transcript captions baked in. Everything drops into WordPress as a draft, into a content sheet as ready-to-schedule, and into Slack as "here's this week's content pack."

The host records once. A full week of distribution appears the same afternoon.

## The Outcome

The per-episode repurposing tax dropped from a full day of a content person to about fifteen minutes of review. Cost per episode fanout is well under a cent. Podcasters who used it stopped feeling like their best conversations were disappearing into the void — the same interview now lived as five different pieces of content across five different platforms. And the audience finally started finding the show from places other than the podcast app.
