## The Story

Every fast-growing company I looked at had the same silent problem. A new hire's first week — the week that decides whether they stay for a year or start looking again in three months — was basically improvised. Someone in HR would frantically stitch together a welcome email, tag the manager on Slack, hope the laptop showed up, and hand the new person a Google Drive folder full of documents from 2019. The new hire spent day one wondering if they'd made a mistake.

The impact was always the same. Ramp-up time stretched from weeks to months. First-thirty-day attrition stayed higher than anyone wanted to admit. And the founder or HR lead ate an entire day per hire manually copying and pasting the same onboarding email into every new inbox.

## What I Built

I built them an onboarding director that runs the first two weeks for the new hire. The moment a hire is confirmed, the workflow generates a personalized welcome package — a warm intro email tuned to their role, a day-one checklist that actually matches their team's stack, links to the specific docs they'll need first, and a schedule of check-ins baked into the calendar. Day three, an AI-drafted "how's it going?" note goes out. Day seven, a milestone email. Day fourteen, a feedback request that lands in HR's sheet automatically. The manager gets Slack pings at the right moments so they don't forget the human parts.

The new hire's first two weeks feel intentional, not improvised. HR stops copy-pasting. Nobody's laptop or 1:1 gets forgotten.

## The Outcome

Onboarding admin per hire dropped from a full day to essentially zero. Ramp-up time compressed measurably because new hires actually knew what to do on day two. First-month attrition dropped for the teams that rolled it out. And the founders finally stopped treating "onboarding" as the thing that gets skipped when the week gets busy.
