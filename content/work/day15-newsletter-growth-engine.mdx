## The Story

Every creator with a newsletter had the same silent leak. Someone would subscribe on the site — excited, curious, real intent to hear more — and then get a generic "thanks for subscribing!" email that could've been written for anyone on earth. First impression, wasted. By the time the next issue landed a week later, the subscriber had already forgotten why they signed up. Open rates on issue two were half of issue one, and it only got worse from there.

Welcome sequences were the obvious fix, but writing a good one takes real time — thinking about the persona, the tone, the sequencing — and most creators never got around to it. The tools that automate it (ConvertKit, Beehiiv sequences) still make the creator write every word themselves.

## What I Built

I built a growth engine that welcomes each new subscriber like the creator actually noticed them. The moment someone joins the list, the workflow reads what it knows — the signup source, any preferences, what they were reading when they subscribed — and generates a personal welcome email in the creator's voice. Then it queues up a short drip that builds context over the first week: a "who I am" note, a "here's the best thing I've written" pointer, a "reply and tell me what you're working on" prompt. Each email is generated fresh per subscriber, not blasted from a template.

The creator writes zero of it. Every subscriber gets the impression they landed in a warm, thoughtful room.

## The Outcome

Open rates on week-two issues stopped collapsing — because subscribers now actually remembered who the creator was by week two. Reply rates on the "tell me what you're working on" prompt turned into a slow stream of real conversations and eventually paying customers. Cost per welcome sequence is a fraction of a cent. And the creators who used it stopped treating "welcome email" as a task on the to-do list — it became a system.
