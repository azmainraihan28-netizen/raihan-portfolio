## The Story

Every content team I worked with had the same expensive ritual. A strategist would pick a target keyword, open ten browser tabs of the top-ranking results, take notes on each, pull out the common headings, guess the search intent, and eventually — three or four hours later — produce a content brief the writer could actually use. That was for *one* article. Teams shipping four or five posts a week were burning an entire strategist just on brief creation.

Agencies felt it worst. Every new client meant a fresh brief queue, and the brief was the thing standing between the writer and the invoice. When briefs slipped, everything downstream slipped.

## What I Built

I built a brief factory. A strategist drops in a target keyword and the workflow does the whole opening ritual for them — pulls the actual live top results, reads what's ranking, extracts the common structure, identifies search intent, and hands the writer back a full brief in under two minutes. Target word count, suggested title options, H2/H3 outline, key questions to answer, competitor gaps, internal linking suggestions, tone guidance. Everything the writer needs to open a doc and start writing well.

The strategist stops being a bottleneck. The writer stops guessing what the brief "probably meant." The client stops waiting.

## The Outcome

Three to four hours of manual research collapsed to about ninety seconds. Cost per brief is under a cent. Content velocity — the number a marketing team actually gets judged on — roughly doubled for the teams that rolled it out. And the briefs themselves ended up more consistent than the human-written ones, because the workflow doesn't skip steps when it's tired on a Friday.
