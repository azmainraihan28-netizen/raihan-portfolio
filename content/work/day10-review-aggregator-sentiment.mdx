## The Story

Every business with a public review presence had the same blind spot. Reviews were coming in on Trustpilot, Google, G2, App Store, wherever — and nobody was reading them systematically. The founder would check once a week, get emotionally hijacked by whichever review had the worst tone, and go silent on the other fifty. Meanwhile the actual signal — what customers keep complaining about, what they love, which product line is trending down — stayed buried.

By the time a real trend showed up in the reviews, it had usually already shown up in the churn numbers. Which is the worst possible order to notice things.

## What I Built

I built them a review radar. Every morning it pulls the latest reviews across their platforms, reads each one, tags the sentiment and the topic — pricing complaint, shipping issue, feature request, praise for support, whatever it is — and rolls the results into a single dashboard. When a negative review lands, Slack pings the team immediately with the reviewer, the platform, the sentiment score, and a suggested response tone. When a positive one lands, it gets flagged as a potential testimonial or case study.

The founder stops opening review sites at all. The team sees the trends in aggregate, responds to fires the same day, and knows what to fix next quarter — because the reviews are finally organized enough to tell them.

## The Outcome

Reaction time on negative reviews went from days to under an hour. The team started spotting product issues weeks earlier because the sentiment trends now sat in a single dashboard instead of scattered across five platforms. Cost per review analyzed is about a tenth of a cent. And the founder stopped losing entire afternoons to one bad three-star that hijacked their mood.
