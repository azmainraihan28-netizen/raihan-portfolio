## The Story

Every professional I talked to had a version of the same morning ritual - coffee, then thirty to forty-five minutes of doom-scrolling through Twitter, Reddit, Google News, three different newsletters, and a stack of tabs they'd never close. The intention was "stay informed." The reality was that by 9 AM, before any real work started, their focus was already gone.

The generic newsletters didn't help - Morning Brew is great, but if you're in AI or SaaS or automation, eighty percent of it is background noise. And the important stuff - a competitor's funding round, a tool launch, a policy shift - usually reached them three days late, from a random Slack message someone else forwarded.

## What I Built

I built them a private morning briefing that runs before they wake up. It quietly pulls the latest articles from whichever sources they care about - tech press, industry blogs, niche RSS feeds, whatever their world actually is - deduplicates the overlap, and hands the whole batch to an AI editor. The editor picks the one story that actually matters that day and explains why it matters, then lists four or five quick hits worth glancing at, one contrarian insight most newsletters won't say out loud, and one concrete action item.

That whole briefing lands in their inbox at 7 AM as a clean, branded HTML email. No apps to open. No tabs to close. Coffee, one email, done.

## The Outcome

Forty-five minutes of morning scrolling collapsed into a three-minute read. Cost per digest is less than a cent, so it runs indefinitely for essentially free. The professionals using it stopped feeling behind and - the surprising part - started catching stories earlier than the people still doing it manually.
