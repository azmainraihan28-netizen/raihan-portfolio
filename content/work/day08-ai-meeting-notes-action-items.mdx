## The Story

Every team has the same conversation at the end of every meeting. "Great call, we'll send notes later." Later almost never happens. And even when it does, half the action items got lost, and the assignees don't quite remember why the thing they got assigned matters. A week later someone asks "wait, who was supposed to handle that?" and nobody knows.

Studies put it at fifty to seventy percent of meeting action items are never completed — not because people are lazy, but because there's no clean bridge between "we discussed it" and "it's a task in the system." Meetings end, momentum drops, and the whole point of having the meeting quietly evaporates.

## What I Built

I built them the missing bridge. When a meeting ends, the transcript — from Zoom, Fireflies, Otter, or just a paste — gets handed off to an AI that reads the whole thing. It pulls out every action item with the assignee, the due date it inferred from context, a priority level, and a two-sentence blurb of *why* the task matters. Then those tasks appear directly in the team's Asana or Trello, already assigned, already dated, already contextualized. In parallel, the meeting summary and key decisions get posted to Slack so anyone who missed the call can catch up in sixty seconds.

Nobody writes recaps anymore. Nobody chases "did you get the notes?" The meeting ends and the follow-through is already in motion.

## The Outcome

Fifteen to thirty minutes of post-meeting writing turned into zero. Completion rate on action items jumped because the tasks appeared in the tool the team already lived in, with full context. Cost per meeting processed is about a tenth of a cent. And the culture shift showed up faster than the time savings did — meetings started feeling worth attending again.
